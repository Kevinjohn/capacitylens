Roles and permissions ​

Choose a role for someone, or find out why a control is missing. Roles are nested: Viewer < Editor < Admin < Owner. The server enforces them; hiding a control in the interface is not the only protection.

Some pages also depend on company settings. Overview access, for example, defaults to Owners and Admins. People without access don't see the link and cannot open the page directly.

Each company has exactly one Owner. Nobody becomes Owner through an invitation or a role change, only through an ownership transfer. An Admin can invite, remove, disable or change the role of any other member except the Owner. Nobody, not even the Owner, can disable or archive the Owner or themselves.

Team & access lists members by role, Owner first. Disabled, archived and removed people move to the collapsed No longer active table beneath. Change a member's role or schedule row covers each action. If a company has no Owner at all, follow A company has no Owner.

Tell sign-ins, members and people apart ​

Three separate things can look like a person in the interface:

  • A sign-in identity: the email and password, or company login, someone uses to reach CapacityLens. One identity can belong to several companies.
  • A member: that identity's role in one company, created by inviting them.
  • A person on the schedule: a row that can receive bookings and time off.

Adding a person doesn't give them a sign-in, and inviting a member doesn't put them on the schedule. Freelancers and contractors can be scheduled without ever getting a sign-in.

Hand the company to someone else ​

Ownership moves in three steps, and nothing changes until all three happen:

  1. The Owner opens Team & access → Company ownership → Manage ownership and nominates an active Admin.
  2. The nominated Admin agrees. Nobody can agree on their behalf. They can decline, or withdraw their agreement before the last step.
  3. The same Owner confirms. They swap: the nominee becomes Owner and the old Owner becomes Admin.

Only those two people see the request. The Owner can cancel it or nominate someone else, which replaces it. A request expires seven days after it is made, and ends if the nominee stops being an active Admin or the Owner stops being the Owner. In every case the company keeps its current Owner. Both people need a recent sign-in confirmation for their step.

Transfer ownership has the full procedure and recovery steps.

Take a role-based tour ​

Anyone can start the tour for their role from Help in the sidebar, even after Getting started is dismissed. Owners, Admins and Editors can also select Show me around on the Getting started card; Viewers don't see that card. The tour opens Schedule first and continues through the guidance for lower roles:

Server-backed companies include an Admin stop about example data; the demo leaves it out. The tour is unavailable while permissions are being checked or can't be confirmed, and while a read-only offline snapshot is open.

View the company as a member ​

An Owner or Admin can see what another active member sees without changing their role. In Team & access, select the eye button on the member's row and confirm. A red Masquerading as name bar stays visible throughout.

You see the company with that member's role and redactions, but you cannot change anything; the server blocks every change. It never applies to another company.

Select End now to return to your own view. Switching company, signing out, a revoked or expired session, a disabled membership on either side, or a server restart also ends it. Starting and ending are recorded in the audit log.

Check what exports and offline snapshots contain ​

An export is redacted the same way as the screen:

What the export containsViewer / EditorAdminOwner
Archived and deleted rowsLeft outIncludedIncluded
Private client/project namesCode namesCode namesReal names

Offline access always behaves like Viewer access: while a cached snapshot is open, nobody can create, edit, delete, import or change membership. A snapshot holds what that person could see when last online, so an Owner's snapshot may contain real private names. Protect the Owner's device accordingly.
