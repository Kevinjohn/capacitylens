How sign-in works ​

Sign in with a password, Google Workspace or Microsoft Entra ID, whichever the person running the server has turned on. The demo and trusted-local mode have no sign-in.

With company login, CapacityLens sends you to Google or Microsoft and never sees your provider password. A provider account alone does not grant access: the first person on an empty installation needs operator approval, and after that each company decides who may join.

Passwords and company sign-in ​

In password-and-sso mode, each configured provider appears once, stacked above the password form with an or use your password divider between them. GitHub is an experimental extra option in this mode only. Provider-only sign-in shows no password form; password-only sign-in shows no provider area.

First-owner setup has its own field order. Microsoft setup, for example, asks for an email address before the provider step so it can run its mailbox proof.

In company-sign-in-only mode, people must use Google or Microsoft. Passwords and GitHub, including older sessions, cannot satisfy it.

Set up Google or Microsoft sign-in covers registration and server settings. Require company sign-in covers switching everyone to a provider.

Invitations and the first Owner ​

On an empty installation, the operator can allow specific email addresses to create the first Google or Microsoft identity. That person follows Set up your company to become its Owner. The allowance closes once the first identity exists.

After that, the Owner chooses Who can join in Team & access: Invitation only (the default), open registration, approved email domains, or approved domains plus invitations. An Admin can see the policy and ask the Owner to change it. Joining through a policy gives Viewer access; an addressed invitation can grant its chosen role. Approved domains match exactly, so team.example.com is not example.com.

To join, open the company's joining link and choose a sign-in method. An existing password user signs in first, then selects Join company. The server admits only identities with current trusted email proof that meet the company's policy:

  • Password signup through an open or domain policy is not available.
  • When email delivery is enabled, a password user can choose Email me a verification link and open it within 60 minutes while signed in to join through an open or approved-domain policy.
  • Microsoft may use its same-browser mailbox check when the address is not already proven.
  • An invitation must be addressed to the verified email.

A company can disable someone's access even when its joining policy allows them.

Connect an existing account ​

  1. Sign in to your existing account and open Account → Security.
  2. Under Company sign-in, choose Connect Google or Connect Microsoft. You may be asked to confirm it's you.
  3. Sign in at the provider with the same email address. Your local email must already be verified.

Connecting keeps your memberships and work. CapacityLens never merges accounts because their email addresses match, and a provider identity already connected to someone else cannot be connected again.

Microsoft may send a one-time link to prove you control the mailbox. Open it in the same browser within 15 minutes, then select Confirm and continue. Returning Microsoft sign-in uses the saved identity and does not repeat the check. Microsoft mailbox proof covers what to do when the email does not arrive or the link expires.

If connecting fails ​

  • You cancel Confirm it's you. No connection starts.
  • Confirmation cannot refresh your session. Sign out, sign in again and retry.
  • Your local email is unverified. There is no email-correction button in Account, and the joining verification link does not verify your local email. Ask the operator: an authorised Owner or Admin runs a guarded repair, which revokes your sessions, so sign in again afterwards. Connect existing accounts describes the repair.

Sessions and sensitive actions ​

A session lasts at most twelve hours from sign-in; activity does not extend it. Thirty minutes without activity also signs you out.

These actions ask you to confirm your identity again when needed: connecting or repairing a company identity, transferring ownership, resetting another member's password, revoking another member's sessions, deleting a company, and importing or purging company data. Invitations, role changes and removing members need only the right role.

If confirming through Google or Microsoft takes you away from the page, return and submit the action again. Confirming does not apply the original change.

Multi-factor sign-in at your provider ​

CapacityLens has no local MFA. If your agency requires MFA, set it up at Google or Microsoft. Enabling company sign-in does not turn it on, and CapacityLens cannot see or enforce the provider's policy or tell whether a challenge happened. Test the policy, its recovery path and session behaviour before relying on it.

When someone leaves ​

  1. Disable their work account at the provider.
  2. In Team & access, remove their membership or select Disable Access.
  3. Select Revoke sessions to end their existing CapacityLens sessions across all their companies. This needs the corresponding authority. Disabling them at the provider does not end those sessions.

In mixed mode, a verified GitHub sign-in is also bound by a Disable Access restriction on the selected address.
