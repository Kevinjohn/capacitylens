Get the schedule ready ​

You set the work. Five steps take a new company to a schedule the team can use. You don't need the Owner or installation guides.

  1. Set the working week. Open Settings. Under Company setup, choose whether work is entered in Hours, Days or Blocks, and which days your company works. Days is the default. Every company setting

  2. Add the people you plan for. Open Resources and select Add resource. Enter a name, set their working days and select Save. They now have a row on Schedule. They don't need a sign-in to be scheduled. Add people to the schedule

  3. Add a client, a project and an activity. Use Clients, Projects and Activities in the left menu, in that order. An activity is the kind of work being booked, such as Design or Development. Prepare clients and work

  4. Book the first work. Open Schedule and drag across some days in a person's row. Choose the project and activity, then select Save. Drag the bar to move it; Undo puts it back. Make the first booking

  5. Invite the team. Open Team & access and select Invite someone. Choose a role, enter their email and pick their row under Link to Resource, so their own work is waiting for them. Select Create invite. Invite teammates

The Getting started checklist on Schedule ticks off steps 2 to 4 as you go, in any order. If the Owner kept the example data, those are ordinary records: delete them from Resources, Clients and Projects when you no longer need them.

Who gets which role ​

RoleCan
ViewerSee the schedule and everyone's work
EditorChange the schedule, people, clients, projects and time off
AdminEverything an Editor can, plus invite people and set access

Most of the team only need Viewer. Give Editor to whoever books work.

Are you an Editor? You can do steps 1 to 4. Inviting people is for an Admin or the Owner. Plan work as an Editor is your day-to-day guide.

Two records, one person ​

Inviting someone lets them sign in. Adding them in Resources gives them a row on the schedule. Most people need both, and Link to Resource joins the two. Neither creates the other.
